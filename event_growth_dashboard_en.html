Event Growth Monitor

—

Are WSDC events growing or shrinking — and where? This tool compares competitor counts at every event with its previous edition and rolls them up by continent, country, state and city. For the current year it compares year-to-date against the same months last year; finished years compare full years. Click the map or the list to drill down, and use the Event trend pills to focus on growing, declining, stable or new events.

Competitors = leaders + followers entered in an event’s WSDC skill-level Jack & Jill divisions (Newcomer through Champion). Age-group, Strictly and other divisions are not counted. When exact entries are missing, the count is estimated from the WSDC points tier and marked est.

Methodology

What this shows

Each row in the data is one event edition in one year: how many competitors entered, and whether that headcount grew or shrank versus the previous edition of the same event.

Competitor counts are leader + follower entries in skill-level Jack & Jill divisions (Newcomer through Champion), taken from official WSDC results. Edition numbers (#N) use the full WSDC history from 1991. Map pins and venue lines use hotel and geo enrichment when available.

The map and list roll those editions up by geography — world → continent → country → state (US) → city — so you can see where the scene is expanding or contracting.

YTD vs YoY

When the selected year is still open (it matches data through and the month is before December), both years are cut to the same months — year-to-date (January through the latest month with data).

Finished years compare full calendar years — YoY. The side panel always says which mode is active.

An open year can look Declining until its autumn events are in — later months are still missing from the data.

Event trend

Every edition gets a status from its own previous edition:

  • Growing / Declining — change of at least 10% and at least 10 competitors
  • Stable — smaller change
  • New — first edition, a return after a gap, or a renamed event (all shown as New)

Event tips show #N after the event name — the edition ordinal across the full WSDC history (from 1991), not only the years loaded in this dashboard. This distinguishes a debut (#1) from a long-running event or a comeback after a gap, even when the growth series is truncated at the data window.

A renamed event starts again at #1 under its new name (e.g. US Open → The Open in 2024; Paris Westie Fest → Paris Swing Classic in 2023), so its first year shows as New rather than as a continuation.

The pills count those editions in the selected area. Click one to keep only that group; All shows the whole area.

Area colors vs event trends

With All, a region’s color is the area trend: total competitors this period vs the same geography last period (YTD or YoY).

With a status pill active, the map instead shows where those events are: darker where more of the selected events took place.

Pink dot next to a region

In the side list, a pink dot after the competitor count means the region as a whole is not Declining, but at least one event inside it is.

Example: Germany can show Stable (or Growing) on the area chip while the pink dot flags shrinking events underneath. Hover the dot for a short reminder; open an event tip or drill into the region to see which editions are Declining.

The marker only appears when the All trend filter is on. It is not drawn on the map fill — only in the list.

Absent & Last edition

Absent (dashed grey chip / grey dashed pin) — the event or region had an edition in the comparison period, but none in the selected window (skip, cancel, move, or ended). On absent rows the chip reads Absent; the Last edition · YYYY tag on the tip gives the year it was last held.

Last edition · YYYY on a map tip means you are looking at that prior held edition, not a current-year result. The Growing / Declining / Stable chip on that tip is the status of that last held edition.

Exact vs estimated competitors

Most headcounts come from official WSDC event results. When a count is missing, the dashboard uses the midpoint of the WSDC points-tier range for that edition and marks it est. in tooltips and the city event list.
