UK WCS Championships

London, United Kingdom · 2009-2026

This article continues a series of WSDC event portraits built from the points registry. After the longest-running event in the United States, we turn to Europe.

Europe first appears in the WSDC registry in 2009, the year three new weekends outside North America launched at once: UK WCS Championships in April in London, City of Light in July in Paris, and World Swing Masters in August in Blackpool. This piece is about the only one of that trio still running today.

From 2009 through 2026, UK WCS Championships ran 13 times, with a long gap from 2020 to 2024. By 2016 it led all European events on every count: dancers who scored there, new dancers, and total Skill Level points. After that its lead slipped away, the pandemic put the weekend on hold for four years, and the event is now working its way back.

Where it ranks among events

Today UK WCS Championships ranks 39th overall and 4th in Europe by dancers with points (752), 20th and 6th by new dancers (211), and 42nd and 5th by total Skill Level points (4,338).

How the event changed

From 2009 to 2016 the event grew almost every year, with a small dip in 2012. In 2017 the numbers turn down, and as it happens the drop starts the year after the UK’s EU referendum. In 2020 the weekend pauses for four years. Europe’s oldest WSDC event returns in 2025, and by 2026 it has already climbed past its pre-pandemic highs.

The best years for new dancers came early on, with 23 to 25 a year between 2009 and 2011. From 2017 to 2019 that number collapsed to just one to three dancers. Since the return, it is climbing again: 19 in both 2025 and 2026.

Who started here

The share of new dancers among those who scored here is high: 211 of 752, about 28%, or nearly one in four. That probably comes down to two things: how much pull the event had in its early years, and a fairly settled group of regulars.

Most dancers who started here are still in Newcomer or Novice: about 42% in Newcomer and another 20% in Novice. Roughly one in five went on to Intermediate and about one in ten to Advanced. Around one in fourteen reached All-Star or higher, and five dancers reached Champion.

Reached Champion:

  • Maxime Zzaoui: first points in 2010
  • Emeline Rochefeuille: 2010
  • Glenn Ball: 2015
  • Virginie Grondin: 2010
  • Coleen Man: 2010

Also worth noting: Ibirocay Alsén and Mathieu Compagnon earned their first points here in 2012.

How often dancers score here

Most dancers with points at this event scored only once: 74% (558 of 752), accounting for 48.5% of all Skill Level points. The chart below breaks down dancers by how many times they scored here, and what share of the points each group holds.

Year over year the picture is different: roughly one in four dancers who score in a given year comes back and scores again the next. The peak was 2016, when 34.7% of the 2015 scorers returned. After the long gap, the 2025 to 2026 return rate was 19.6%.

The event’s most successful dancers

By total points, Melanie Zeltner leads (40). Next are Sarah Cook (37), Emeline Rochefeuille and Ekow Oduro (36 each), and Oceane Garcia (35).

The most scoring appearances: 9 each for Emeline Rochefeuille and Steve Hall. Emeline Rochefeuille and Thibault Ramirez share the lead in wins with 4 apiece.

The most successful pairing is Thibault Ramirez and Karin Kakun, who placed together twice and won both times, in Advanced and All-Star. Two more pairs made two finals together and won one of them: Steve Hall with Emeline Rochefeuille, and Arnaud Perga with Izabella Kowalska. Arnaud Perga with Emeline Rochefeuille, and Ekow Oduro with Ardena Gojani, also placed together twice but never won.

Closing

UK WCS Championships was among the first WSDC registry events outside North America. For years it was a landmark for Europe’s strongest dancers and helped shape how West Coast Swing grew on the continent. The post-Brexit decline and a four-year pandemic pause cost it the top of the European rankings, but the 2025 return and a strong 2026 suggest that Europe’s oldest event can once again be one of the most sought-after weekends on the calendar.
