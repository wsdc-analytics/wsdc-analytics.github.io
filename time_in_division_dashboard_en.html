Time in division

Updated —

How long does it take a dancer to earn the right to move up — and how long do they wait before they actually do? This tool answers that for every WSDC division: months and events from the first point to the May or Must threshold, the share who have already scored in the next division, and the pause before that first next-level point. Switch between Table, Scatter, Density and Dynamics; on Scatter and Density you can also run a Timeline month by month. The ⓘ buttons and Methodology explain each number.

Methodology

What this shows

Every row in this tool is one dancer in one division and one role — Leader and Follower are tracked separately, so a dancer who competes in both roles appears twice. We follow each of them from their first point in the division to the moment the WSDC rules say they may move up (the lower threshold) or must move up (the upper one), and then on to their first point in the next division.

Example. Anna scored her first Novice point as a Follower in March 2019. By October 2020 she had enough points to be allowed into Intermediate (May). She kept dancing Novice, reached the Must threshold in April 2021, and scored her first Intermediate point in September 2021. Her Novice spell: 18.9 months and 9 events to May; pause after May 11.5 months.

We only show spells whose threshold was reached in the years when both thresholds existed in the rules: Novice, Intermediate and Advanced from 2018, All-Stars from 2021. Earlier points still count toward months and events — the year floor only decides which spells are displayed. Data cut-off: —.

Filters & KPIs
  • Division and Role pick the spells. In Dynamics, Role becomes a View: Overall draws one combined line, Leaders & Followers draws two lines so you can compare roles.
  • Threshold switches every number between the May and Must milestone.
  • From / To year limit the Table, Scatter and Density to spells whose threshold was reached in those years. Dynamics always shows the full span. In Timeline (Scatter / Density) the year range is replaced by the month scrubber — see Timeline below.

The cards on the left summarise whatever is currently filtered:

  • Dancers — how many spells match.
  • Median months / Median events — the typical time and number of events from the first point to the threshold. Half of the dancers were faster, half slower; medians are not pulled up by a few very long spells.
  • Share next — of those who reached the threshold, how many already have a point in the next division.
  • Median pause — among those who moved up, the typical wait between the threshold and their first next-level point.

In Timeline the same cards update for the month under the scrubber. Share next and Median pause only count dancers who had already entered the next division by that month — not anyone who moved up later in the data.

Reading the cards. Suppose Novice · Follower · May shows Dancers 412 · Median months 8.1 · Median events 5 · Share next 63% · Median pause 6.4. A typical Novice Follower in that selection needed about eight months and five events to be allowed up, roughly two in three have since scored in Intermediate, and those who did usually waited about half a year.

Scatter

One dot per dancer. Left to right: months from the first point to the threshold. Bottom to top: events it took. When several dancers land on exactly the same spot, the dots are arranged in a small ring so each one stays visible. Dashed lines mark the median months and events for the current filter.

Hover a dot to see who it is, the dates of their first point and threshold, and — if they have already moved up — when they entered the next division and how long the pause was.

Reading it. A dot far to the right but low on the chart is a dancer who took years but only a handful of events — someone who competes rarely. A dot high and to the left is a dancer who crossed quickly by attending many events in a short time.

Density

The same picture as Scatter, but dancers are grouped into bins of 6 months × 2 events. The darker and larger the bubble, the more dancers fall into that bin. Use it when the Scatter is too crowded to read.

Hover a bubble to see the bin range in plain words, how many dancers are in it, and what share of the current selection that is.

Example. A bubble labelled 6–12 months · 4–6 events · Dancers 84 · 21% of selection means 84 dancers — about one in five — reached the threshold between six months and a year after their first point, scoring at four or five events along the way.

Timeline

On Scatter and Density, switch from Static to Timeline to watch the same picture build month by month. Instead of From / To year, a scrubber runs from the division’s year floor (January 2018 for Novice, Intermediate and Advanced; January 2021 for All-Stars) through the latest month in the data. Each frame shows every spell whose threshold was reached on or before that month — a running cumulative, not a single-month slice.

The chart axes stay fixed to the full cumulative range so the field does not jump when a late outlier appears; dashed median lines and the KPI cards move with the scrubber. Play steps one month at a time (×1 is the default pace; ×0.25 is slower, ×2 faster). Pause keeps your place; Reset returns to the floor. Changing a filter, or leaving Timeline and coming back, also resets to the start.

While fewer than 10 dancers are on screen, medians and pause are shown as a dash — the same stability rule as in Dynamics. Hover still works during Play: if the point under the cursor disappears on the next frame, the tooltip closes.

Reading it. Novice · May · Timeline at As of Jan 2019 with Dancers 615 means 615 Novice spells had already crossed May by that month. Drag forward and watch the cloud fill in; Share next rises only when those dancers later score in Intermediate no later than the month you are on.

Dynamics

One KPI as a line over calendar years. Click a card on the left to choose which one. For Dancers, Median months, Median events and Share next the year is when the dancer reached the threshold. For Median pause the year is when they entered the next division, and the pause is measured back to the threshold.

A gap in a line does not mean there is no data — it means fewer than 10 dancers that year, too few for a stable median or share. Dancers itself is drawn for every year with at least one spell.

Example. Median pause for Novice Followers reads 2023 → 7.5: dancers who scored their first Intermediate point in 2023 had typically waited seven and a half months since reaching May. If 2019 shows no point, fewer than ten Followers entered Intermediate that year.

Table & CSV

Every filtered spell as a row, sorted by months, then events, then name. Rows are tinted by role. The columns:

  • First point — date of the event where the dancer scored their first point in this division and role.
  • May reached / Must reached — date of the event where they crossed the selected threshold.
  • Months, Events — the distance between those two events.
  • Entered next — date of their first point in the next division, same role; blank if it has not happened yet.
  • Pause, mo — months from the threshold to Entered next.

Dates are the start day of the event. Download CSV exports the whole filtered list (not just the visible page), with the same date columns as the table.

Example row. Anna Example · Follower · Novice · 2019-03-15 · 2020-10-09 · 18.9 · 9 · 2021-09-24 · 11.5.

How we count months and events

Months are the number of days between the start dates of two events, divided by the average month length (30.44 days) and rounded to one decimal. Two points at the same event count as 0.1 month, not zero. When an event has no known dates, we fall back to counting calendar months inclusively (March to July = 5).

Events are distinct competitions where the dancer scored in this division and role — the first-point event and the threshold event both count.

Pause runs from the threshold event to the first event in the next division. Some dancers move up before they reach the threshold, for example All-Stars who enter Champions by petition; for them we count from their last point in the division instead, so the pause is never negative.

All-Stars before 2021 had no formal Champions rules, so only Champions points counted; from 2021 either Champions or All-Stars points can satisfy the threshold. Advanced points expire on a rolling 36-month window — that window is still checked in calendar months, exactly as the WSDC does.

Example. First Novice point at an event starting 15 March 2019, May threshold at an event starting 9 October 2020: 574 days ÷ 30.44 = 18.9 months. Between them Anna scored at seven more events, so Events = 9.
